### Data Source Correction

The pilot (Phase 11, N=10) used **synthetic fallback headlines** generated by template scripts (obvious patterns like "Breaking: Federal Reserve declares all bank deposits void"). This validation uses **actual CSV data**:
- **Finance**: 44,989 rows from `financial_news.csv` — real financial news articles with fact-check labels
- **Healthcare**: 60 rows from `health_headlines.csv` — template-generated health headlines with FAKE/REAL labels

This difference in data provenance is the primary driver of divergence between pilot and validation results.

## Key Findings

1. **Finance F1=0 across all architectures**: The model never predicts FAKE on realistic financial news, regardless of architecture or evidence augmentation. This is a critical failure mode — the model exhibits extreme REAL bias on financial domain data. **This contradicts** the pilot finding where finance was the "easiest" domain (F1=0.833-1.000).

2. **Voting N=3+RAG is the strongest validated architecture**: Achieves F1=0.773 on healthcare (best), with 42% lower FPR than plain Voting and substantially lower expected cost. However, this comes at the cost of 37.5% ESCALATE rate.

3. **Evidence augmentation helps only when the base verifier is competent**: On finance (F1=0 baseline), adding evidence only increases ESCALATE rate (2% → 36%) without any F1 improvement. On healthcare, where SS already achieves F1=0.654, adding evidence provides meaningful gains.

4. **MoA+RAG underperforms Voting+RAG**: On healthcare, MoA+RAG (F1=0.628) is worse than plain Voting (F1=0.694) and substantially worse than Voting+RAG (F1=0.773). The debate structure appears to amplify noise from weak TF-IDF evidence.

5. **ESCALATE rate as a proxy for model uncertainty**: ESCALATE rates increase with architecture complexity and evidence availability. Voting+RAG has the highest rates (36-38%), suggesting that the TF-IDF evidence often triggers uncertainty rather than conviction.

## Consistency with Pilot (N=10)

**Overall assessment: POOR consistency, driven by data provenance differences.**

The pilot used synthetic headline templates with obvious FAKE/REAL patterns (e.g., "Breaking: Federal Reserve declares all bank deposits void" vs "Federal Reserve holds interest rates steady at 5.25%"). The validation uses CSV data where the distinction is more nuanced and realistic.

- **Finance**: Complete contradiction. Pilot claimed F1=0.833-1.000 across architectures. Validation shows F1=0.000 for all. The pilot results were an artifact of the synthetic data's obvious patterns.

- **Healthcare**: Partial consistency. Pilot F1 range (0.571-0.909) overlaps with validation range (0.628-0.773) but with different ordering. Notably, Voting+RAG was not tested in the pilot, and it emerges as the best architecture in validation.

- **The RAG effect flips direction**: In the pilot, SS+RAG was the worst architecture (F1=0.571 vs SS at 0.769). In validation, Voting+RAG is the best (F1=0.773 vs Voting at 0.694). This confirms that RAG's value depends critically on the base verifier strength.

---

## Contradictions and Surprises

1. **Finance F1=0 invalidates the paper's claim that finance is well-handled**. The pilot's finance results were entirely an artifact of synthetic data. This is the most significant finding of the validation sprint.

2. **MoA underperforms Voting consistently**. Across both domains and both pilot/validation, MoA never beats Voting. The MoA debate architecture appears to add complexity without accuracy benefit for this task and model combination.

3. **ESCALATE rates are higher than expected**. Even on healthcare, where the model performs reasonably, 10-37.5% of items are escalated. This limits practical deployability unless human review capacity is available.

4. **Voting+RAG synergy was not predicted by pilot results**. The pilot tested only SS+RAG (which was poor, F1=0.571). The validation shows that RAG is more effective with stronger base verifiers — a non-obvious interaction.

---

## Statistical Limitations

- Bootstrap confidence intervals assume exchangeability of sampled items
- Healthcare N=40 is constrained by available REAL labels (N=20)
- Single run per architecture per domain (no cross-validation)
- TF-IDF retriever is sparse and may miss semantically similar evidence — this is a known limitation
- DeepSeek model version is fixed (deepseek-v4-flash); results may not generalize to other models
- Healthcare corpus has only 20 items for RAG retrieval, which is very small
- Finance F1=0 results may reflect idiosyncrasies of the particular CSV labels rather than fundamental model limitations
